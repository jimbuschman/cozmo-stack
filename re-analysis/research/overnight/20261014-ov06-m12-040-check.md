## Omission (behaviour-changing): dock actions that survive Cancel(−1)

- **Why an action can survive:**
  - ActionList::Cancel cancels nothing while the list's clearing flag is set (0x0053DE18..0x0053DE1E);
  - ActionQueue::Cancel's pending loop stops at the first DeleteActionAndIter result ≠ 1 (0x0053E6E4);
  - actions queued from a callback during the cancel are not covered.
- **Where survivors die:** in ActionList::Clear (0x00511146), then ~ActionList (0x00511156), through ActionQueue::Clear
  (0x0053F96A..0x0053F9B0).
- **What follows:** an IDockAction dying there can send a **second, conditional AbortDocking** after AbortAll's, and
  resets docking +0x10/+0xC before the raw free.
- **Candidate crash (M1-034 standing rule):** by then BehaviorManager is destroyed and robot+0x44 is null
  (0x00511124..0x00511130). The IDockAction dtor calls RemoveDisableReactionsLock through it (0x00557520..0x0055753C if
  the action started; 0x00557554..0x00557570 if +0xC4 ≠ 0), and the callee reads [this+0x4C] (0x005A3A84). That is a
  null read.
- **Reachability: UNKNOWN.** It depends on DeleteActionAndIter's return and guard (0x0053F9E4..), owned by M8-015, which
  overnight OV18 is reading.

## Contradicted

cancels `_pending` and clears `_pending` and `_active`. The native AbortDocking writes nothing. Its evidence also omits
the return-value contract (0x0063BE3A, 0x0063BE52). It's reopened to IMPLEMENTATION_GAP: make the tagged method match,
or show the extra cancellation is owned and unreachable.
