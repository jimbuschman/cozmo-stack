# OV33 — pet/face/speech lifetime recipients

Answers `requests/overnight/20261014-ov33-m14-013.md`. Main was pulled at `2990bf32` before reading request and manifest. Primary binary: local ARMv7 `resources/lib/armeabi-v7a/libcozmoEngine.so`, SHA-256 `02263c07f6bb60f4d7f351a3667dca84fd3e6c0fc6f838e18b5de7cf4e2989e1`. Thumb disassembly used Capstone skipdata, resolved PLT symbols and vtable entries. Ghidra output and earlier reports were navigation aids; instruction addresses below are the evidence. Research only, no hardware or code/manifest edits, no shipped uploads. Proposed classifications require manager review and production comparison.

The main finding is the speech ordering: clearing the component's executor does not execute work still in its member queues, but a batch already moved into the worker's local vector can finish. Stop joins the worker before the provider, mutex and speech bundles are destroyed. Replacing this with “cancel everything and immediately free speech data,” or with “drain every queued synthesis request,” changes the native lifetime behavior.

## Reachability, policies, build rows and remaining boundaries

These owners are ordinary Robot components. Robot teardown is reachable when the normal engine removes a Robot; it does not require SDK/CodeLab. They are not run on every connected tick. Pet/face nested allocation cleanup requires corresponding nonempty state; empty/null owners follow the explicit skips. Speech creates its concrete queue during construction, independent of having any spoken text. In-flight speech additionally requires a previously queued CreateSpeech whose batch has escaped to the worker. App requests may supply text/activation, but the engine owns cancellation/join order. A queued sleep action is not established as a call to these owner destructors; use its actual ActionList path rather than relabeling teardown as sleep.

M1-034 applies to an actual null queue at Dispatch::Stop's dereference `0x007FB900` or a null held handle at `0x004EF1BE`; the normal constructors/owners shown do not establish those states as reachable. If encountered through a terminating/undefined path, skip the smallest failed operation visibly with M1-034 and owning record, retain prior valid state. Do not turn the defined pose verification-and-continue paths into exceptions or skips. Stop's partial-failure catch/throw behavior requires the remaining exception trace before prescribing any additional policy; no speculative deadlock workaround is proposed.
